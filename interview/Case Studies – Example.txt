Yes — that example is giving away too much.

For a **candidate-facing pre-interview example**, it is better to keep it **very simple and generic**, so candidates understand the **format** and the **type of thinking expected**, without being coached toward your real interview topics.

A better approach is to use a lightweight example like a **simple internal Q&A tool, document helper, or workflow assistant**, and frame it around how they would think about building a small **RAG or agent demo/tool** end to end.

Here is a cleaner version you can use.

# MRO AI Candidate Interview

## Case Studies – Example

## Introduction

As part of the interview, you will be asked to review and discuss a **case study** describing an AI tooling or workflow automation scenario.

This document provides **an example only**.
The **actual case study will be different**, but the **format and expectations will be similar**.

The purpose of the case study is to understand how you approach the design of an **end-to-end AI solution**, such as a **RAG application**, a **simple AI tool**, or an **agent-based workflow**, from an **AI Tech & Tooling perspective**.

---

## Interview format for the case study

The case study discussion will take place within a **50-minute interview**.

The expected format is:

* **5 minutes** to read and think about the scenario
* **10–15 minutes** to talk through your initial approach
* **20–25 minutes** of follow-up discussion and deeper questions from the interviewers
* **5–10 minutes** for discussion on delivery approach, trade-offs, and prioritisation

You may make notes on paper if helpful, but **not on an electronic device**.

---

## What we are looking for

Please consider the example from an **AI Tech & Tooling / solution design perspective**. For example:

* How you would approach building the solution **end to end**
* What architecture, workflow, or components you would prioritise
* How you would think about **data, prompts, retrieval, tools, orchestration, evaluation, and controls**
* Where a **simple RAG pipeline** might be enough, and where an **agent-based approach** may or may not be justified
* What trade-offs, risks, or delivery challenges you would want to understand

The aim is to understand how you **approach practical AI solution design and delivery**, even in unfamiliar situations.

There is no single “right” answer — we are interested in your **reasoning, judgement, and prioritisation**.

---

## Clarifications

* By **AI Tech & Tooling perspective**, we mean thinking about whether the proposed solution is:

  * appropriate for the problem
  * technically sound and feasible
  * robust, controllable, and supportable in practice
  * designed in a way that can be evaluated, improved, and operated over time

* You do **not** need to cover everything — focus on the areas you think are **most important**

* You do **not** need specialist banking knowledge

* We are **not** expecting production-ready code in the interview, but we do expect you to think in a way that reflects how you would build or lead delivery of a solution in practice

---

# Case Study Example — Internal Document Help Tool

## System outline

* A team wants to build a small internal AI tool to help users ask questions about a set of internal documents.
* The documents are stored in a few common formats such as PDF, Word, or web pages.
* The tool should return short answers and, where possible, point users to the relevant source material.
* The team is considering whether a **simple RAG-based design** would be enough, or whether a slightly more **agent-like workflow** is needed.
* The solution is still at an early stage, and the design has not yet been finalised.

## Your task

Describe the **main considerations** you would focus on when designing this solution from an **AI Tech & Tooling perspective**.

You might consider, for example:

* How you would approach the solution **end to end**
* What the main components of the solution would be
* What data preparation or document processing might be needed
* Whether a **simple RAG approach** would be sufficient
* In what situations an **agent or tool-using workflow** might be useful
* How you would think about **testing, evaluation, and controls**
* What you would prioritise for an **initial MVP**
